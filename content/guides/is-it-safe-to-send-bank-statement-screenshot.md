It's reasonably safe if you've verified who's asking and you hide what they don't need first: the full account number (keep the last four digits), card numbers and unrelated transactions. Never send one to someone who contacted you out of the blue. A landlord doesn't need your Social Security number before you agree to rent. [RedactPal](/redact-bank-statement/) hides those for you, free in your browser.

This guide is general information, not legal or financial advice. Several sources are from the United States and are marked as such. The principles apply anywhere.

## What can someone do with your bank statement?

A statement carries more than a balance. It can show your full name, address, account number, sort code or routing number, your employer (from salary lines), where you shop and who you pay.

The US Federal Trade Commission says thieves who get your credit card or bank account numbers can use them to buy things and to open new credit in your name ([FTC, "What To Know About Identity Theft"](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-identity-theft), Sept 2024). Real transaction details also make a phishing message or a phone call sound convincing, because the scammer can quote your own merchants back to you.

None of that means you should refuse. It means the file should contain only what the reader needs.

## Who legitimately asks for a statement, and what they need

| Who asks | What they usually want | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Landlord or letting agent | Evidence of income or funds | Ask what exactly they need. Hide the full account number and unrelated transactions |
| Mortgage lender | Full statements for the period they specify | Send what the lender asks for. In the US, Fannie Mae's rules list what a statement must show (see below) |
| Visa or immigration authority | Often statements for a set period | Follow the authority's own checklist. Don't redact fields it lists |
| Employer or payroll | Rarely a full statement | Ask why they need it and what for |
| Accountant | Full statements | Use their secure portal, not chat |

For mortgages, Fannie Mae's Selling Guide (section B3-4.2-01, effective 05/04/2022) says a bank statement must identify the institution and the borrower, show at least the last four digits of the account number, show the period covered, include all deposits and withdrawals, and show the ending balance. It also accepts statements that the borrower downloaded from the internet ([Fannie Mae](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-4.2-01/verification-deposits-and-assets)). Two things follow. Showing only the last four digits of the account number is acceptable to this lender. And for a mortgage, you shouldn't remove transactions: the rule asks for all deposits and withdrawals.

## Red flags that it's a scam

The FTC's page on rental listing scams ([June 2026](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/rental-listing-scams)) gives signs that apply well beyond rentals:

- Someone contacts you and asks for personal or financial information. The FTC's advice is to never give it to anyone who contacts you.
- A landlord asks for your Social Security number or other sensitive details before you've agreed to rent. The FTC says a landlord doesn't need your Social Security number until then.
- You're told to pay by wire transfer, gift card or cryptocurrency.
- You're pressured to decide quickly. The FTC's advice is to walk away.

Check the person first. Look up the agency or company yourself and contact it with details you found, not ones in the message.

## What to hide and what to keep

| Hide | Keep |
| --- | --- |
| Full account number (keep the last four digits) | Your name, if the reader needs to match it to an application |
| IBAN, sort code or routing number, unless the recipient needs them to pay you | Bank name and statement period |
| Card numbers | Income or salary lines when you are proving income |
| Email addresses and phone numbers | Ending balance when you are proving funds |
| Unrelated transactions: medical, legal, personal | Everything a mortgage lender or authority lists as required |
| Your full home address, if the reader doesn't need it | |

Never change a figure, a date or a name. Redaction covers information up. It doesn't rewrite the document.

## Screenshot or PDF: which is safer?

Either can be safe once it is redacted. A downloaded PDF is the better document. It is complete and dated, and it shows the institution and period, which lenders check for. Fannie Mae's rule for downloaded statements asks that they identify the institution and the source of the information.

A screenshot is easier to make and easier to get wrong. It can include notification banners, other account names, or your phone's clock and battery, and it often crops the date range. If a screenshot is all you have, check the whole frame, including the edges, before you send it.

## How to send it

1. Use the recipient's secure upload portal if there is one.
2. Otherwise use email to an address you verified, or an encrypted messenger.
3. Avoid public links and shared folders that anyone with the URL can open.
4. After they confirm receipt, delete it from the chat, and ask them how long they keep it.

## Already sent it to a scammer?

Act today:

1. **Call your bank** using the number on your card or its website. Say which details were shared, and ask them to watch the account.
2. **Check recent activity** for payments you don't recognize. The FTC lists withdrawals you didn't make and charges for things you didn't buy as possible signs of identity theft ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-identity-theft)).
3. **Consider a fraud alert or credit freeze (US).** The FTC says an initial fraud alert is free, lasts one year and tells businesses to check with you before opening a new account. A freeze means nobody can open new credit while it is in place. You contact the bureaus to place them. In other countries, ask your bank or national consumer agency for the equivalent.
4. **Report it.** In the US, report identity theft at IdentityTheft.gov and scams at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov). Elsewhere, use your national fraud-reporting service and your local police.
5. **Change passwords** on the accounts that share an email address or phone number with the bank.
