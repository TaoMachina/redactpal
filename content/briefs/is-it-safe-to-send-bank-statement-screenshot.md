## H1
Is it safe to send a screenshot of your bank statement?

## Direct answer (40–60 words)
> It's reasonably safe if you've verified who's asking and you hide what they don't need first: the full account number (keep the last four digits), card numbers and unrelated transactions. Never send one to someone who contacted you out of the blue. A landlord doesn't need your Social Security number before you agree to rent.

## Outline
- **H2: What can someone do with your bank statement?** Name, address, account and sort/routing numbers, spending patterns, and employer from salary lines. FTC: identity thieves use account and card numbers to buy things and open accounts. Phishing gets easier with real transaction details. Keep it factual and don't fear-monger.
- **H2: Who legitimately asks for a statement, and what they need** A table: landlord (proof of income/funds), mortgage lender (full statements; Fannie Mae list), visa/immigration (often full statements; check the embassy's list), employer (rarely, so ask why), accountant (full, via a secure portal).
- **H2: Red flags that it's a scam** FTC rental-scam signs: asked before viewing or agreeing, pressure, wire/gift card/crypto payment, unsolicited contact.
- **H2: What to hide and what to keep** Hide/keep table; link the landing page.
- **H2: Screenshot or PDF: which is safer?** The PDF is complete and dated (lenders accept downloaded statements); a screenshot may show notifications or other accounts. Either way, redact first.
- **H2: How to send it** Use the lender's portal where available; otherwise email or an encrypted messenger; avoid public links. Delete it from chat history afterwards where possible.
- **H2: Redact it in 30 seconds** RedactPal auto-boxes IBANs, card numbers and emails; drag over transactions (CTA).
- **H2: Already sent it to a scammer?** Contact your bank, consider a fraud alert or credit freeze (FTC), and report it (reportfraud.ftc.gov in the US; give non-US equivalents generically).

## Facts to include (verified)
- FTC "What To Know About Identity Theft" (Sept 2024): thieves can "buy things with your credit cards", "get new credit cards in your name"; place a fraud alert with one bureau; consider a credit freeze; review bank statements regularly. https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-identity-theft
- FTC "Rental Listing Scams" (June 2026): "Never give personal or financial information to anyone who contacts you"; "Until you've agreed to rent a place, a landlord doesn't need your Social Security number"; wire transfers, gift cards and crypto are scam indicators; "If anyone pressures you to make a decision quickly, walk away." https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/rental-listing-scams
- Fannie Mae B3-4.2-01 (05/04/2022): statements must show the institution, the borrower, "at least the last four digits of the account number", the period, all deposits and withdrawals, and the ending balance; downloaded statements are acceptable. https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-4.2-01/verification-deposits-and-assets
